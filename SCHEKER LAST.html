Private by design

The audit stays on your machine

A privacy tool that phoned home would be a contradiction. So this one doesn't.

Everything runs locally

Trackers, cookies and policy checks are computed in your browser from what the page already loads. No page content is ever sent anywhere.

No accounts, no ads

No sign-up, no analytics, no third-party SDKs, no advertising. It never identifies you and there's nothing to log in to.

One optional request

The only network call it can make is a tool-list refresh — off by default, to a URL you choose. That's it.

Why it matters: under the US CLOUD Act, data held by US providers can be compelled regardless of where the servers sit — which is exactly what the Schrems rulings put in question for European data. Sovereignty Checker makes that exposure visible, then points to a European option.

Questions

Good to know

Is it really free?

Yes. It's free, independent, and contains no ads or upsells. If you'd like to support it, star the repository or share it with your team.

Do you collect any of my data?

No. The scoring happens entirely in your browser and no browsing data is transmitted. Your settings are stored locally on your device. The only outbound request the extension can make is an optional tool-list update to a URL you set. See the full privacy policy.

How accurate is the hosting detection?

Trackers, cookies and privacy-policy detection are read directly and reliably. Data flow and infrastructure are heuristics based on the domains a page loads — Google, Meta and AWS versus Hetzner, OVH and STACKIT. They indicate likely US versus EU involvement, not a definitive hosting audit, and the score is a fast signal rather than legal advice.

Which browsers are supported?

Any Chromium browser — Chrome, Edge, Brave, Opera, Arc. A Firefox build is straightforward from the same code; see the project README.

Can I add or correct a tool?

Absolutely. The database is a single file, and the extension can auto-update from a hosted list, so new tools and fixes reach users without a reinstall. Contributions are welcome.

Privacy Policy

Last updated: January 2026

Short version: the extension does not collect, transmit, or sell any data. All analysis happens locally in your browser.

What the extension accesses

To score a page and suggest alternatives, the extension reads, locally and only in your browser:

  • the current page's address and the third-party resources it loads (to count trackers and classify infrastructure),
  • cookies visible to the page (to count them),
  • links and visible text on the page (to detect a privacy-policy link and GDPR mentions).

This information is used only to compute the on-screen score and is never sent anywhere. It is held in memory for the active tab and discarded when the tab is closed or reloaded.

What is stored

Your settings and any per-site "don't show" choices are stored with the browser's local extension storage on your device. If you enable auto-updates, a cached copy of the tool database is stored the same way. None of this is transmitted to us or anyone else.

Network requests

The extension makes no network requests by default. If you explicitly enable "Auto-update the tool list" and provide a URL, the extension will fetch that db.json file to refresh its list of tools. That is the only outbound request it can make, and it contains no personal data.

No tracking, no ads, no third parties

The extension contains no analytics, no advertising, and no third-party SDKs. It does not create an account or identify you.

Your rights (GDPR)

Because the extension does not collect or process personal data on any server, there is no personal data of yours for us to access, export, or delete under Articles 15–17 GDPR — none is ever received. Data that stays on your own device is governed by your browser's own storage and sync settings, which you control directly.

Changes to this policy

If this policy changes, the "last updated" date above will change too. Material changes will also be noted in the extension's release notes.
